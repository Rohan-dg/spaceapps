# Decisions log

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Moon-fixed frame = IAU 2015 rotation model from astronomy-engine (`RotationAxis`) | Built into the required library; matches Horizons' MEAN_ME frame to <0.004° in our validation. |
| 2 | Sun aberration applied (first order), Earth light-time/aberration ignored | Effects are ≤0.006° / ≤0.0002°, much smaller than the terrain uncertainty. |
| 3 | Spherical Moon, R = 1737.4 km | LOLA reference sphere; DEM heights are relative to it. |
| 4 | Network: the NASA hosts were blocked by the sandbox policy; created a personal environment allowing them | Needed for the real DEM and Horizons validation. |
| 5 | The Horizons API host is `ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/api/horizons.api` (the brief's `ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov` is the documentation host and returns 404 for the API) | Verified by fetching the docs. |
| 6 | Validation and precompute scripts are TypeScript run with `tsx` (not .mjs/.py) | Reuses the exact same math module as the app, so one code path is validated. |
| 7 | "Earth visible" = Earth centre above terrain horizon; "sunlit" = Sun centre above horizon, disk fraction also stored | Simple, explainable, and standard in the illumination literature, which uses disk fraction. |
| 8 | DEM: LOLA `LDEM_80S_40M` (40 m/px, polar stereographic) for sites poleward of 82°S; `LDEM_16` (16 px/deg, ~1.9 km/px) for Chandrayaan-3, Apollo 11 and Chang'e-4 | 40 m is the brief's 20–60 m/px class and covers every polar site plus 150 km of terrain around it (462 MB). The global product is enough where the Sun climbs well above the terrain. |
| 9 | Horizon ray range is 150 km, not the brief's 30–50 km | Distant massifs matter at grazing angles. Malapert, for example, is 5 km high and ~120 km from Shackleton, and still stands ~0.4° above the curvature-dropped horizon. Steps grow with distance (max(res/2, 0.3%·d)). |
| 10 | Exact spherical geometry for terrain elevation angles, not the bare d²/2R term | It equals the d²/2R formula to second order and stays exact at 150 km. |
| 11 | Rim/summit sites whose cited point lands on a slope get `snapRadiusM`: the analysis point moves to the highest DEM cell within that radius (Shackleton rim 600 m, Malapert 280 m, de Gerlache rim 5.0 km, Faustini rim 4.5 km). The cited and used coordinates are both shown | A point 140 m down an inner crater wall is not where anyone would land. Two of these coordinates were approximations to begin with (`verify: true`). |
| 12 | The series is stored as run-length encoded visibility plus base64 Int16 elevations (0.01°) and a Uint8 solar-disk fraction, about 350 KB per site for 3 years at 30 min | Run-lengths make sunset/sunrise countdowns an O(log n) binary search, and the file loads lazily per site. |
| 13 | Sanity rule "rims are highly lit" applies only within ~1.6° of the pole | Nobile (4.6° out) and Faustini (2° out) are expected to be ~45–50% lit, consistent with the literature. |
| 14 | Verdicts look forward one synodic month (29.53 d) from the selected time; gaps cut by the window edge are shown with "≥" / "(at least)" | "Percent of the lunar day" needs a defined window, and a forward window answers "what if we land now?" |
| 15 | Verdict reasons describe the *middle* of the longest gap (edges trimmed by 15%) | At a gap's edges the body is by definition on the skyline, which made reasons say "−0.8° vs −0.8°". |
| 16 | Countdowns refine the 30-min series crossing by bisection on the live ephemeris (~30 s precision) | Cheap (a dozen ephemeris calls) and avoids ±30 min jitter in the headline numbers. |
| 17 | Early deploy = a private claude.ai Artifact of `dist/`; GitHub Pages workflow and `vercel.json` are included but need the repo owner to enable Pages / import into Vercel | The session may only push to its feature branch, and Pages deploys from `main`. |
| 18 | Compare panoramas share one vertical scale only when it doesn't stretch any scenario's range by more than 1.8× | An equatorial site's 70° Sun would otherwise flatten polar terrain into a line. |
| 19 | P2 custom coordinates: a 400 m/px block-mean of LDEM_80S_40M (6.2 MB as base64 JSON, `public/dem/`) is ray-marched in a Web Worker (TS port of the Python pipeline), then the full 3-year series is computed in the browser (~3 s). Labelled "approximate" everywhere | 200 m would be 18.5 MB. A unit test measures the 400 m horizon against the 40 m pipeline: mean error 0.10–0.60°, max 2.4° on the narrow connecting ridge. That number is stated in the UI. |
| 20 | P2 3D view: per-site 24 × 24 km height tiles (161², 150 m spacing, curvature included) from the 40 m DEM; three.js with shadow mapping lit from the true Sun direction; lazy-loaded chunk | It shows *why* a site is lit or shadowed. The caption notes that terrain beyond the tile isn't drawn and that the panorama is authoritative. |
| 21 | P2 export: CSV/JSON of the current scenario, with a download where the browser allows it and a copy-able text box otherwise | Sandboxed hosts (e.g. the Artifact preview) block downloads. |
| 20b | 3D for any point: custom coordinates build the tile in the browser (`terrainTile` in raymarch.ts, a port of `write_terrain_tile`) from the 400 m DEM already used for their horizon; non-polar listed sites get a 120 × 120 km, 121² tile from LDEM_16 | The 400 m DEM is already shipped, so no new data for custom points. A 24 km tile on a 1.9 km DEM would be ~13 px across, so the global tiles cover a wider area. |
| 22 | All binary data (series, browser DEM, 3D tiles) ships as base64 inside JSON | Static hosts (and the Artifact host) reliably serve JSON. Gzip on the wire recovers most of the 33% base64 overhead. |
| 23 | Visual redesign: warm paper theme (`#F4F0EA` / charcoal ink) plus a true dark theme from one token set, with one accent (oxide orange) for interaction and the time cursor. Type is Urbanist 700–800 for display, IBM Plex Sans 300/400 for body, IBM Plex Mono for every number. Square 2 px corners, hairline rules instead of card grids, an asymmetric site index / work area, Lucide icons, and no glows, gradients or decorative motion | A planning instrument should read like a survey sheet, not a dashboard template. Sun ochre and Earth blue are data colours, checked with the dataviz palette validator in both themes (all checks pass). |
| 24 | Metric cards replaced by one ruled Sun-vs-Earth readout table; verdicts sit in a narrow column beside it | Twelve identical cards hid the pairing. A table puts Sun and Earth side by side for every quantity. |
| 25 | 3D view rebuilt as one always-on Moon (globe, south polar cap, site window) instead of a per-site 24 km tile; the old Terrain3D component is removed | The full Moon stays in view at every zoom, so the site keeps its context. Each layer hides where a finer one takes over, so there is one surface, not stacked tiles. |
| 26 | Cast shadows are ray-marched in the fragment shader on the height textures (horizon method with curvature, Sun disc as a soft edge), not shadow maps | Shadow maps cannot cover 1 m to 3500 km in one scene. Marching the same heights the horizon pipeline uses keeps the picture consistent with the numbers. |
| 27 | Detail below 5 m (craters, rocks, regolith) is procedural, fades in only when a pixel is smaller than the data, and is labelled and switchable | The brief asked for ground-level detail; no measured data exists at that scale. Making it visible as illustration is honest; hiding the label is not. |
| 28 | Type is the system SF Pro stack (not bundled), and icons are original SF-Symbols-style SVGs; Lucide removed | SF Pro and SF Symbols are licensed for Apple platforms only, so they are referenced, not shipped. Non-Apple systems fall back to their UI fonts. |
| 29 | Launcher-style restyle: dark first, Inter + JetBrains Mono, hairline-bordered 12 px panels, pill segments, list rows that highlight like a menu, one warm red accent; a ⌘K command menu and a bottom action bar with key hints | Every site, preset and view is one search away from the keyboard, and the hints teach the shortcuts. Data colours (Sun, Earth, good/warn/poor) are unchanged. Inter replaces the SF stack because it is open-licensed and renders the same on every OS. |
| 30 | The 3D globe streams real-terrain meshes cut from the CGI Moon Kit `ldem_64` (5° tiles, 321² corner-registered samples, true scale, LROC 16k colour) around the view once the screen resolves finer than about 1.5 km/px; the globe hides itself under drawn tiles through a 72 x 36 mask | The single 4096-wide height map behind the globe is about 2.6 km per sample, so zoomed-in ground was a texture on a smooth ball. Tiles keep the download proportional to what is on screen (about 75 KB per tile) instead of shipping 530 MB of heights. |
| 31 | Colour and normal textures load with flipY = false | The shaders put north at v = 0, like the height DataTextures. With the loader's default flipY the colour and relief were mirrored north-south against the heights and the Sun, which flattened and misplaced shading. |
| 32 | Antialiasing is supersampling (TAARenderPass: 2 to 4 jittered samples per frame while moving, accumulating to 32 when still), and procedural sub-texel detail is off by default | MSAA only smooths triangle edges; the flicker came from shading inside triangles. Averaging sub-pixel samples removes it at any zoom, and nothing invented is shown unless asked for. |
| 33 | Single click on the surface probes without moving the camera; double-click (any point or site marker) flies to a near-horizontal close-up (8° above the ground, distance 60 samples of the finest data there) and only that point's neighbourhood gets full-detail terrain; a marker click selects without flying | Clicks were easy to trigger while orbiting and threw the camera around. A low, horizontal view shows relief and shadows the way a lander would see them, and limiting full detail to the clicked area keeps the cost bounded. |
| 34 | A frame-time governor picks one of three quality levels (pixel ratio 0.75 / 1 / up to 1.5, shadow-march steps 50 / 75 / 100%, tiles on screen 12 / 20 / 28). Moving frames use one sample per pixel; antialiasing accumulates one sample per frame once still. Globe and cap meshes are halved and ordinary tiles mesh every 4th sample | Slower laptops could not hold a frame rate with supersampling on every frame. Per-pixel shading comes from the height maps, not the mesh, so the lighter meshes do not change the picture, and a still view still reaches 32 samples per pixel. |
| 35 | Declutter pass: no explanatory sentence under each panel title, no red accent bar on panel titles, no 'Landing site' overline, no keyboard-hint chips on the mode tabs or the bottom bar, no Preset dropdown in the header (presets stay in the Ctrl/⌘ K menu), no red page glow, and the Compare 'best' marker is bold value plus a grey label instead of a red bar and pink tag | The repeated card-plus-caption template and decorative chrome made the app read as generated rather than designed. Titles and data now carry the page; shortcuts are listed once, in Help and the command menu; interaction hints that remain (click the 30-day strip to jump) moved to tooltips. |
